# Principles and workflows for reproducible science

**Course:** K9F5740 — *Quality Assurance in Research From a Global Perspective*  
**These sessions:** two guided self-study mornings + two Team-Based Learning afternoons

## Purpose

These two days start from **what reproducible science means** ([The Turing Way](https://book.the-turing-way.org/reproducible-research/overview/overview-definitions/): recreate the work from the **same data and the same analysis**). It's important to note from the beginning that there are many definitions of reproducible science, and that the meaning of the concept varies depending on discipline. For example, in qualitative research, the focus of reproducible science is more related to [auditability](https://www.keele.ac.uk/korsa/qualitative/reproducibilityreplication/)

, then teach everyday **workflows** that support those aims: a durable project folder, documentation, versioning, sharing boundaries, and responsible AI use.

You organise a project folder and write documentation for **one of the studies included in your own PhD projec**, then transfer that text into the  Swedish Research Council DMP template as part of your final assignment.

**Success criterion — the 5-year test:** someone returning in five years (you or a colleague) can understand what was done and which decisions were made. KI also requires documentation to be kept for at least 10 years, or as long as the data is available.

## Learning outcomes (these two days)

By the end of the two days, you can:

1. **Explain** The Turing Way definition, note where definitions diverge (including qualitative auditability), and relate that to KI’s research and documentation guidelines.
2. **Structure** a durable project folder and its documentation (README, decision log, naming and versioning) so the project passes the 5-year test.
3. **Track** changes and decisions in a form humans and tools can both read, and justify which versioning approach fits your project.
4. **Draft** SRC DMP answers on **documentation**, **data description**, **data quality**, **storage**, **security**, and **who is responsible**.
5. **Judge** what can be shared, what must stay protected, and how to use KI-supported AI tools (Copilot-first) responsibly — with disclosure and verification.

## How the days are organised (TBL)

Mornings are **guided self-study** on Canvas (about 3 h). Afternoons are **Team-Based Learning** (about 3 h).

| Phase | What happens | Your job |
|-------|--------------|----------|
| Pre-work (morning) | Required readings and short prep tasks | Finish the morning pack **before** 13:00 |
| iRAT | Individual readiness test on that day’s reading | Answer alone; closed book as instructed in the room / Zoom |
| tRAT | Same items, as a team | Argue to consensus; use the reading, not guesses |
| Clarification | Short facilitator follow-up on splits | Ask about items the team disputed |
| Application exercises | Significant problems on *your* PhD project (Day 1 App 1 uses shared synthetic folders first) | Produce the folder / history / DMP paste the exercise asks for |
| Share-out / peer critique | App 1: short findings on a shared board (Padlet or similar). App 2: inter-team critique against the 5-year rubric | Post clearly; give and take critique against the rubric, not taste |

We do **not** use classic simultaneous report for these applications (packages differ; Day 1 is hybrid). Readiness tests still run as normal TBL team scoring.

## What we expect from you

**Before each afternoon**

- Complete that day’s **required** morning items (definition/AI on Day 1; Part B and sharing on Day 2).
- Skim the [Session → SRC DMP map](assignment-1-dmp.md) so you know where paste steps land.
- Bring notes on **your** PhD project (folder location, who may see what, open questions). Day 1 App 1 is the only shared synthetic case.

**During the afternoons**

- Join on time (Future and/or Zoom as listed). Keep cameras on in Zoom when you can; use breakout rooms when directed.
- Treat incomplete projects as normal. Psychological safety is part of the design: messy first drafts are expected.
- Follow the AI rules from Day 1 morning before any Copilot (or other tool) use: no personal/patient/sensitive research data in prompts; keep prompt + output; verify; disclose to your team. Manual drafting is always allowed.
- No real personal or patient data on shared screens, boards, or in examples you post.

**After Day 2**

- Leave with a project package plus partial SRC DMP drafts (six areas) and, if you used AI, a disclosure note.
- Submit only through the **course** assignment path — not to this session team. Checklist: [After Day 2](after-day-2-checklist.md).
